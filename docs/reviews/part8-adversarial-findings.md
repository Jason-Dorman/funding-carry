**What is different from the Part 7 run, and it is the point.** That run left *fourteen of thirty* findings unverified when it hit its own cap, and its file had to warn the reader that an unverified finding is one agent's unchecked claim. This run verified **every** finding. Each was given two independent skeptics with different methods — one reads the code and the documented contract, one copies the repository and runs an experiment — and where they disagreed a third did both and had to name the specific mistake one of them made. Five findings needed that tiebreak. A finding survived only on a majority failing to kill it, and skeptics were told to default to *refuted* when unsure, which is why twenty of thirty-three did not survive.

Duplicates across lenses are merged rather than kept, with the merge count recorded: seven independent lenses reaching the same shutdown defect is itself a finding, and the count is the honest way to say so.

## The one that mattered most

`Initiator.run` closed a "we are shutting down" channel **before** `initiator.Stop()`, and `deliver` selected over that channel and the report handoff. Once a channel is closed both cases of a `select` are ready whenever the buffer has room, and Go chooses between them at random — so roughly half the ExecutionReports arriving during the logout were logged and thrown away while the consumer was still draining and the buffer was still empty. quickfix acknowledges a report as soon as `FromApp` returns, so every one of those was acknowledged too and could never be resent.

That is the exact opposite of this part's second acceptance criterion, and of ADR-0020's own decision 2. Seven of the nine lenses reached it independently. The skeptics measured it rather than argued it: a unit probe delivered 470 of 1000, and twelve SIGTERM shutdowns over a real socket lost an already-accepted ExecutionReport four times, once the `FILLED` itself. The regression test reproduces it at **515 dropped out of 1000** handoffs that would all have succeeded.

The fix is that abandoning a report is a **timeout**, which cannot fire while the handoff would succeed, rather than a signal, which races it. ADR-0020 decision 2 is amended to say so, and to record what the original wording got wrong: an always-ready case in a `select` is not a fallback, it is a coin flip.
